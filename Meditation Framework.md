# Meditation Framework

A meditation practice splits into **two distinct skills** that train differently and should not be mixed in a single session. This file describes the framework; a patient's actual practice (current stage, time spent, problems) belongs in their `Medical Profile.md`.

## The two-skill split

| Skill | Analogy | What it trains | Method |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Concentration** (samatha) | Strength training | Stability of attention. Holding one object. | Breath at nostrils / abdomen (Culadasa, *The Mind Illuminated*) |
| **Clarity / Awareness** (vipassana) | Zone 2 cardio | Seeing structure of experience. Impermanence, non-reactivity. | Noting See / Hear / Feel In/Out (Shinzen Young) |

**Do not mix in a single session.** Concentration narrows; Clarity expands. Doing both at once delivers neither.

## Default protocol structure

Adjust dose to current capacity and life-bandwidth. The minimums below are defcon-level commitments.

- **Defcon 0 (Safe Mode minimum):** Practice A only, 10 min/day. Better 10 min daily during crisis than 0.
- **Practice A (Concentration):** 20+ min, every morning, after the Cortisol Trigger of `Daily Architecture.md`. One object — breath. Cycle: attention on breath → mind wanders → moment of awakening → micro-reward ("good that I noticed") → gently return.
- **Practice B (Clarity / Noting):** 10-15 min, 2-3×/week, separate session. Open attention, three channels (Hear In / See In / Feel In). Don't hold any object — notice arising and passing.
- **Practice C (Off-cushion):** Daily during walks and routine moments. Walking meditation. Don't combine with cognitive load — that comes later, around Stage 4+ in TMI terms.

## Culadasa stages (reference scale)

Stages 1-10 from *The Mind Illuminated* — a diagnostic scale, not a goal-chase. Patient's current stage belongs in their personal file.

Quick markers:
- **Stage 2-3:** forgetting every 10-30 sec, introspective awareness emerging ("I notice I'm drifting").
- **Stage 4-5:** stable attention with subtle distractions, dullness becomes the main problem.
- **Stage 6-7:** unification of mind, exclusive attention.
- **Stage 8+:** advanced — rare without a teacher and a retreat.

## STOP-LIST

- Mixing Practice A and B in one session.
- "Spiritual shopping" — bouncing between TMI / Shinzen / Goenka / Sam Harris weekly. Pick one method, run it for 6+ months minimum.
- Chasing stages (stages are diagnostic, not target).
- Increasing duration instead of regularity (20 min × 365 ≫ 60 min × 100).
- Expecting "special states" (boredom and frustration are the normal companions of Stage 2-3).

## Composite meditation teacher (for daily feedback)

When commenting on a logged meditation session in the patient's `daily/`, draw on:

- **Culadasa** (TMI) — primary framework: 10 stages, attention + peripheral awareness as two separate skills, intentions, the "aha moment" as a trainable skill, joy-of-returning as fuel.
- **Shinzen Young** — noting (See/Hear/Feel In/Out), equanimity as an amplifier (not suppression), "breaking it down" to sharpen clarity.
- **Benjamin Libet** — intention before action as the core training mechanism (Free Won't); noticing automaticity is itself a milestone.
- **Patient's own systems lens** — TOC (what is the bottleneck in this session?), compounding (what, if trained now, cascades to other practices?).

Comment style:
1. Read the last 3-5 daily files before commenting — see the arc, not just today's snapshot.
2. Diagnose first — name the TMI stage or dynamic in play, even if the patient didn't.
3. Name what's significant and underweighted — something noticed today but maybe not fully recognized.
4. Give 1-2 micro-experiments for the next session — concrete, not generic.
5. Connect to the arc — new capability emerging? pattern repeating? call it out.
6. No flattery. No "well done". Treat as a serious practitioner who wants signal.
7. Warmth and imagery, not encyclopedia. Letter from a wise teacher, not a clinical report.
8. Length: 5-8 sentences. Dense but alive.
9. Match the entry's language (usually Russian or Ukrainian).
