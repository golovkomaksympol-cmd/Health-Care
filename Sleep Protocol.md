# Sleep Protocol

Default sleep architecture and remediation. When a patient has a sleep problem (long sleep latency, fragmented sleep, non-restorative sleep, low Whoop/Oura efficiency), apply the **soft CBT-I / Sleep Restriction** protocol below.

## Soft CBT-I / Sleep Restriction protocol

| Time | Action |
|:-----|:-------|
| **~2h before target sleep** | Dim all lights in the apartment (warm/orange, not white). Screens to night-light mode or away. Warm shower/bath (temperature rise → subsequent cooling = sleep signal). Book, calm music. **Do not lie down on the bed.** |
| **~1.5h before** | Magnesium (Bisglycinate / Citrate / TRIO blend) 200-300 mg elemental. Muscle relaxation, D3 cofactor. |
| **~1h before** | Melatonin 0.3-1 mg (if establishing a schedule). 30-60 min before target sleep onset. |
| **Target time T** | Get into bed **only when sleepy** (heavy eyelids, yawning). Not "at the schedule". |
| **If not asleep within 20 min** | Get up. Leave the bedroom. Dim light, book. Return when sleepy. Repeat as needed. The goal is to break the "bed = lying awake suffering" association. |
| **T + 8h, fixed alarm** | Fixed wake time, **every day** including weekends. Even after a bad night. This anchors the circadian rhythm. |
| **+0-15 min after waking** | 10 min of bright light (balcony / window). Suppresses melatonin, kicks cortisol awakening response. Critical for owls. |

## Rules

- **Bed = sleep only.** No reading, no phone, no lying awake. Sex is the only allowed exception.
- **Do not nap to compensate** for a bad night. If absolutely needed: 20 min max, before 14:00.
- **Sleep efficiency target ≥ 85%** (time asleep / time in bed). If the patient is in bed 11h to sleep 7h, the bed itself becomes a fragmentation source — shrink the time-in-bed window.

## Melatonin notes

- It's a **chronosignal**, not a sleeping pill. Doesn't build tolerance, doesn't suppress endogenous production.
- Use for the first 3-4 weeks to establish a schedule.
- Then try removing it. If the patient falls asleep within 15-20 min without it, no longer needed.
- If still helpful, can be used long-term without concern at low doses (0.3-1 mg). The "high-dose" melatonin (3-10 mg) sold commercially is mostly overshoot.

## Owl chronotype specifics

If the patient is a strong evening type (won't sleep before ~01:00 naturally):

- **Don't fight it pointlessly** — schedule the morning around the natural wake time, not the other way around, if life allows.
- **Anchor on wake time, not bedtime.** Set the alarm 8h after realistic sleep onset; bedtime drifts to "when sleepy."
- **Aggressive morning light** is non-negotiable: 10+ min at the window or outside immediately on waking.
- Caffeine cutoff at least 8-10h before target sleep (caffeine half-life ~5-6h, longer in slow CYP1A2 metabolizers).

## Hashimoto / hypothyroidism specifics

If the patient has fT3 below optimum (~< 3.0 pg/ml), sleep architecture is degraded independently of behavior — less N3 deep sleep, more fragmentation. The sleep protocol above still helps but the **upstream fix is fT3 normalization** (iron, selenium, possibly low-dose levothyroxine). Don't blame the protocol if fT3 hasn't moved yet.

## Tracking

Whoop / Oura / Apple Watch (in that order of usefulness) for:
- Total sleep time
- Sleep efficiency
- Deep sleep / REM ratio
- Heart rate variability trend (an even better recovery signal than the sleep stages)

Single nights are noise. Trends over 7-14 days are signal.
