# Modern Food Environment

The parent food model. **Thesis: the default path feeds you badly.** Follow the crowd — eat what's available, cheap, palatable, and default — and the modern environment lands you at *overfed and undernourished*. Health here is a **low-entropy state you must actively maintain against a gradient**; stop spending attention and you decay to the environment's statistical mean, which is disease.

## Mechanism

- **Your diet is the output of your food environment, not your intentions.** Behaviour follows the path of least resistance — you eat what's around, easy, and default. So the real question is not "what's optimal?" but "**what does my default deliver?**"
- **Two regimes, opposite failure modes:**
  - **Ancestral / village regime:** the environment's default *is* reasonably healthy — whole foods, set routines, seasonal, minimal processing. You can run on **autopilot** and land near "healthy." Failure mode = **scarcity** (under-eating energy, micronutrient gaps, infection).
  - **Modern industrial regime:** the default is **ultra-processed (UPF)**. Autopilot lands you at **excess energy + refined sugar + refined fat + low fibre + low protein-per-calorie**, hyper-palatable and energy-dense, with the food **matrix pre-destroyed** (fast absorption → high glycemic/insulin load). Failure mode = **excess *and* deficiency at once** — overfed, undernourished.
- **It's the processing, not just the nutrients.** The **Hall 2019 (NIH metabolic-ward RCT)** fed UPF vs unprocessed diets **matched for calories, sugar, fat, fibre, macros**, ad libitum — subjects spontaneously ate **~+500 kcal/day** on UPF and gained weight. Hyperpalatability + destroyed matrix → **passive overconsumption**, independent of the nutrient numbers. UPF is now ~50–60% of calories in Western supermarkets.
- **The entropy framing (the model's spine):** with **no energy input** (no attention, no understanding), a system relaxes to its environment's most probable state. In the modern food environment that state is the population disease statistics (obesity, metabolic syndrome). *"Following the crowd" = maximum entropy = the average outcome.* Escaping it costs energy — but the energy can be **spent once**, by re-engineering your local defaults, instead of paid per-meal as willpower.

## Scale of simplicity

Nutrition science is an intractable tangle — every nutrient, every study contradicting the last. This model coarse-grains **all of it** to two variables: **(1) is this the *default/crowd* choice or a *chosen* one, and (2) how processed is it (NOVA)?** At that scale "what should I eat?" collapses to a single heuristic: **treat the default as guilty until proven innocent; eat food the environment did little to.** That one rule replaces reading every label and adjudicating every macro.

**Usefulness test:** drop to nutrient-by-nutrient optimisation (macro ratios, micronutrient targets) and the system is hopelessly complex again — you lose. The model only pays off at the **degree-of-processing / default-vs-chosen** scale.

## Cynefin domain

- **Mechanism (UPF → passive overconsumption → metabolic harm):** **Complicated** and reasonably ordered — the processing→overeating link is demonstrated (Hall RCT).
- **Outcome (what is *the* optimal diet / does this make *this* person healthy):** **Complex** — individual, contested, emergent. The model's power is that it **doesn't need to predict the optimum** — it just moves you off the bad-default attractor, which is the high-value, low-controversy move.

## Time horizon (Lyapunov)

The environment's pull is **constant and daily**; the entropy drift is **slow** (weeks→years of creep). So this isn't a one-time fix — it needs **continuous small inputs**. The leverage move: convert vigilance into **structure** — re-engineer your kitchen/pantry so the healthy choice is again the *default* (rebuild the village locally). Then autopilot works *for* you and the maintenance energy drops toward zero. **Instrument your defaults (what's in the house), not your willpower.**

## Practice

- **Treat the default as guilty.** In a normal supermarket, a *random* basket ≈ UPF. Don't choose passively.
- **Shop the perimeter / whole foods; cook** (cooking = processing *you* control).
- **Re-engineer your local environment:** what's in the house is what you eat. Make the good choice the path of least resistance; remove the bad default from arm's reach.
- **Anchor on protein and fibre** — the two things the modern default under-delivers.
- **Spend the understanding-energy once** (set routines/defaults), then coast. Don't rely on per-meal willpower — that's paying the energy tax forever.

## Confidence

- **High:** UPF dominates the modern supply; UPF drives passive overconsumption (Hall RCT); environment/default beats intention as a behaviour driver.
- **Medium:** how much is "processing per se" vs the nutrients UPF happens to carry; the exact NOVA boundary is fuzzy.

## Falsification

**Attack 1 — "'ultra-processed' is an unscientific, circular category; it's just calories/sugar/fat."** NOVA *is* criticised as imprecise (some UPF — whole-grain bread, plain yogurt — are fine). → **Survives, qualified:** the **Hall metabolic-ward RCT matched the nutrients and still got +500 kcal/day on UPF**, so processing itself (matrix/palatability) carries independent effect. "Avoid UPF" is an excellent *heuristic* even though NOVA is fuzzy at the edges — use it as a rule of thumb, not a precise law.

**Attack 2 — "the village is romanticism; traditional diets had stunting, deficiency, short lives."** True. → **Qualified:** we are **not** claiming the ancestral world was healthier overall — only that its *food default required no vigilance* (its failure mode was scarcity, not processed excess). The transferable point is the **default**, not nostalgia.

**Result — survives as a framing/heuristic model, both claims qualified.** It doesn't name the optimal diet; it reliably gets you off the bad default, which is where most of the risk is.

## Boundaries

- **A framing model, not a nutrient prescription.** It gets you off the bad default; it does **not** settle *which* whole-food pattern (Mediterranean vs low-carb, etc.) — that's downstream, individual, and lives in other models / the patient profile.
- **NOVA/UPF is fuzzy at the edges** — don't wield it as a precise classifier.
- **Assumes access & affordability.** Part of *why* the modern default is bad is that healthy costs more money/effort — the "spend energy to escape" move is not equally available to everyone (equity boundary).
