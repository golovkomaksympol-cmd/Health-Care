# Fiber

The fill-the-rest lever of a whole-food diet, and the one that's **hardest to quantify**. Protein has a number (g/kg), fat is roughly "don't restrict quality" — but "enough fiber" resists gram-counting. This model's job is to give fiber a **tractable sufficiency signal.** Payload for the microbiome lives in `Gut Microbiome.md`; this is the food-side lever, parented by `Modern Food Environment.md`.

## Mechanism

**What fiber *is*:** carbohydrate that human enzymes **can't digest** in the small intestine, so it reaches the colon intact — where it's either **fermented** by bacteria (soluble/fermentable) or **passes through** (insoluble). (Lignin is the convention-based exception — a non-carbohydrate polyphenol; inulin/FOS are oligo-, not poly-saccharides.)

**Two functional classes (Lustig's split) — you need both, and they do different jobs:**

- **Soluble (gel-forming):** dissolves in water → viscous gel. Psyllium, β-glucan (oats/barley), pectin (apple/citrus), inulin, guar. Effects: **slows gastric emptying + blunts the glucose/insulin curve**, **binds bile acids → lowers LDL/ApoB** (see `ApoB and Atherosclerosis.md`), and the fermentable ones (inulin, pectin, β-glucan) feed bacteria → SCFA. *Psyllium is mostly viscous/bulking, only partly fermented.*
- **Insoluble (bulk/lattice):** doesn't dissolve, doesn't gel. **Cellulose, hemicellulose, lignin** — wheat bran, whole grains, vegetable/fruit skins, nuts, seeds, legumes. Effects: **bulk + faster transit (laxation)**, mechanical. Less fermentable.

**Why both, together (the Lustig barrier idea):** insoluble fiber forms a mesh/lattice along the gut wall; soluble fiber plugs it into a **gel barrier** that slows sugar absorption (delivered lower down, smaller spike) and lets some carbohydrate **reach the colonic bacteria** instead of being absorbed high up. Refining strips both → sugars hit fast and bacteria starve. *(Mechanism is real in outline but popularized — hold at Medium.)*

**The supplement asymmetry (the user's/Lustig's point, corrected):** supplements are almost all **soluble** (psyllium, inulin). Insoluble fiber and its benefit are **bound to the intact food matrix** — you get it only by eating whole plants. (Wheat bran exists as a quasi-supplement, so "literally impossible" is slightly too strong — but directionally right: *you can't pill your way to real fiber; you eat plants.*)

**Third category — resistant starch (RS):** the *fraction* of starch that escapes small-intestine digestion and ferments in the colon — so it **behaves like fermentable fiber** (a MAC, strong butyrate producer), though chemically it's starch. **Best source by far is legumes** (RS trapped in cell structure) — *not* the much-hyped "cook-and-cool rice/potato" trick, which converts only a few % of starch, trims the glucose spike slightly, and does **not** make rice low-carb. Eat beans for RS; don't reorganize the kitchen around cooling rice.

**The standout whole food — legumes.** Beans, lentils, chickpeas deliver **soluble + insoluble + resistant starch + protein + polyphenols in one package**, low GI. If any single food anchors the fiber pillar, it's legumes (chickpeas → hummus, etc.).

## Scale of simplicity

"Am I getting enough fiber?" is **uncomputable per-gram** — dozens of fiber types, unlabeled, in every plant, with individual fermentation. This model refuses the gram scale and coarse-grains to **two countable/observable signals:**
1. **Input — plant *diversity*, not grams:** aim for **~30 different plant types a week** (American Gut Project: >30/week → markedly more diverse microbiome than <10). Diversity automatically captures soluble + insoluble + resistant starch + polyphenols that **no single gram-target or supplement gives.**
2. **Output — read the stool:** Bristol type 3–4, regular, formed, no straining = enough fiber + water. Insoluble fiber's job (bulk/laxation) is *directly readable* from the toilet.

**Usefulness test:** dropping to gram-counting or per-fiber-type optimization is below-scale — uncomputable and unsustainable. The model pays off at the **diversity-in / stool-out** scale.

## Cynefin domain

- **Mechanism (soluble → gel → slowed glucose + bile binding; insoluble → bulk; fermentable → SCFA):** **Complicated** — ordered, derivable.
- **Outcome (does more/which fiber make *this* person healthier):** **Complex** — depends on the individual's microbiome and tolerance. Instrument (stool, then labs), don't forecast.

## Time horizon (Lyapunov)

- **Stool/regularity:** responds in **days** — the fastest instrument.
- **Microbiome shift:** **weeks→months** (`Gut Microbiome.md`).
- **Metabolic (glucose, LDL/ApoB):** **weeks**.
- **Hard endpoints (CVD, T2D, colorectal cancer, mortality):** **decades** — don't forecast; steer off the near-term proxies (stool → labs).

## Practice

- **Don't count grams.** Target **~30 different plants/week** — diversity is the tractable proxy for both fiber types + MACs + polyphenols.
- **Eat plants whole** (matrix intact) — the insoluble fraction and its benefit come *only* with real food; a supplement can't replace them.
- **Cover both classes** via variety: soluble (oats, legumes, fruit, psyllium) + insoluble (whole grains/bran, vegetable skins, nuts, seeds).
- **Legumes = the best single fiber food** (soluble + insoluble + resistant starch + protein + polyphenols in one) — make beans/lentils/chickpeas a staple (e.g. hummus). This is also the top resistant-starch source, far more than any cook-and-cool trick.
- **Psyllium is a targeted tool, not a substitute** — add it for a *specific job* (LDL/ApoB, glucose blunting, stool normalizing), not to "hit fiber."
- **Instrument = stool** (Bristol 3–4, no strain) + **water** (fiber without hydration backfires → constipation).
- **Ramp slowly**; tolerance is individual (see Boundaries).

## Confidence

- **High:** the soluble/insoluble functional split; fiber-*rich whole-food diets* → lower CVD / T2D / colorectal cancer / mortality (Reynolds 2019, *Lancet*, dose-response); plant diversity → microbiome diversity; stool as a readout.
- **Medium:** the exact optimal gram target; the Lustig gel-lattice barrier detail (plausible, popularized); magnitude of soluble-fiber LDL effect.
- **Low:** isolated fiber *supplements* for disease prevention (see Falsification).

## Falsification

**Attack 1 — "just supplement fiber, or hit a gram target."** Strongest counter that *supports* the food-first stance: **fiber-supplement RCTs failed to prevent colorectal adenoma recurrence** (Polyp Prevention Trial / wheat-bran trials, Schatzkin & Alberts 2000) — while fiber-*rich diets* consistently associate with lower risk. → **The model survives and sharpens:** it's **whole-food fiber (matrix + diversity), not isolated supplements**, that carries the benefit. Directly vindicates "eat plants, don't pill it."

**Attack 2 — "fiber isn't essential; low-fiber/carnivore people do fine, and cutting fiber relieves some IBS-C/bloating."** True in the narrow sense — fiber isn't required for survival, and in specific GI conditions less can be better short-term. → **Qualified:** for the *general* goals (microbiome diversity, metabolic/CVD/cancer risk over decades) the evidence favors adequate whole-food fiber; but **don't dogmatize — instrument by symptoms**, and honor individual tolerance. This is why the model's target is a signal (stool), not a mandate.

**Result — survives, food-first and instrument-led.** "More fiber always" is too strong; "diverse whole-food plants, read the output" holds.

## Boundaries

- **A sufficiency-heuristic, not a prescription** — it tells you *whether* you have enough, not a gram quota.
- **Individual tolerance varies:** FODMAP-sensitivity, SIBO, active gastritis/IBD can make certain fibers (esp. fermentable or coarse insoluble) irritating → ramp slowly; specific tuning lives in the patient profile, not here.
- **Fiber needs water** — high fiber + low fluid → constipation, the opposite of the goal.
- **More is not linearly better** — very high intakes bloat; benefit curves plateau.
- **Supplements ≠ food fiber** — psyllium/inulin fill a soluble niche only; they don't deliver the insoluble/matrix/diversity payload.
- **Defers to `Gut Microbiome.md`** for the fermentation/SCFA payload and to `Fermented Foods.md` for the live-microbe (not fiber) lever.
