# Skeletal Muscle Hypertrophy

## Mechanism

Maximum muscle growth requires **simultaneous activation of two signals on mTORC1** (AND logic, not OR):

- **Amino-acid signal:** leucine → RAGULATOR / Rag GTPases → mTORC1. Threshold ~2.5-3 g leucine ≈ ~40 g of high-quality animal protein per meal. Time profile: peak at 1-2 h, then refractory ~3-5 h.
- **Mechanical signal:** high-intensity loading (close to failure, RIR 0-3) → integrins / FAK → mTORC1. Time profile: active 24-48 h, declining. Activation is **local** — only in loaded fibers.

Insulin is a third route into mTOR (PI3K → Akt), but it's systemic and primarily **anti-catabolic** (inhibits breakdown) rather than anabolic. Food without training does not build muscle through this path.

Leucine also stimulates insulin secretion, so protein activates mTOR through two paths at once.

## Scale of simplicity

The complex system is muscle growth — a tangle of mTOR/mTORC1 branches, mechanotransduction (integrins/FAK), satellite-cell dynamics, and the moving balance of protein synthesis vs. breakdown. No practitioner can steer that signalling web directly. This model makes growth simple by coarse-graining one level up, to **the muscle as the unit of analysis** (contrast strength/power/skill, whose unit is the *movement pattern*). At that scale the whole tangle collapses to one short, controllable heuristic: **sufficient mechanical tension + adequate volume near failure + enough protein + recovery → the muscle grows.**

The lever lives at the scale of the muscle, not the molecule: you manipulate training volume and effort (and protein/recovery), never mTOR or leucine signalling per se. The molecular story only justifies the heuristic — it is not where you operate.

**Usefulness test:** the model earns its place only by making growth tractable at the muscle scale. If at that scale the system still looks complex — you find yourself reasoning about mTORC1 branches or satellite-cell timing to make a training decision — you've dropped below-scale and the model has stopped paying off. Don't go there; go back up to volume/effort or drop the model.

## Cynefin domain

- **Mechanism (mTORC1 signaling): Complicated.** Ordered and predictable — cross the leucine threshold + apply mechanical load and MPS rises acutely. Cause→effect is knowable and reproducible in the lab.
- **Outcome (actual hypertrophy in a given human over months): Complex.** Emergent from sleep, recovery, hormones, genetics (fiber type, receptor density), adherence, and cumulative load. Predictable only in hindsight — never lend the mechanism's confidence to the whole-person growth outcome.

## Time horizon (Lyapunov)

- **Hours** for the acute MPS response (leucine peak 1-2 h; mechanical signal 24-48 h). Predictable — this is the window the mechanism actually forecasts.
- **Weeks-to-months** for measurable hypertrophy: prediction weakens; treat direction as likely but magnitude as uncertain.
- **Beyond ~3 months:** individual response diverges — **stop forecasting, start instrumenting** (track strength progression, circumference/DEXA, log adherence; adjust from measured signal, not from the model).

## Practice

- **Minimum ~40 g protein per meal.** Less = leucine threshold not crossed; partial MPS only.
- **3 meals per day inside a 10-12 h feeding window** — 3 MPS windows instead of one.
- **One of the meals within 1-2 h of the strength session.**
- **High-effort training (RPE 8-10) is mandatory.** Without the mechanical signal, protein delivers only partial MPS.

## Falsification

**Attempt 1 — is a discrete leucine "threshold" the real driver, or just total load-volume near failure?** Strongest counter-evidence: mechano-biology and volume-response work (e.g. Schoenfeld's dose-response meta-analyses, and studies showing sets-to-failure drive growth even with modest or distributed protein) argue that **proximity-to-failure and weekly set volume dominate**, and that MPS can be maximized across a fairly wide protein-per-meal range rather than at a sharp ~2.5-3 g leucine cliff. Later distribution data (e.g. even 100 g protein in one meal is largely used, ~0.4 g/kg/meal being "enough" rather than a hard 40 g wall) further blunts the threshold framing.
**Result:** model **qualified, not falsified.** The mechanical/volume signal is confirmed as necessary and arguably rate-limiting — which this model already encodes as mandatory (RPE 8-10, AND-logic). The leucine "threshold" survives as a **soft saturable curve, not a hard cliff**; ~40 g is a practical floor, not a magic number. Downgrade any claim that the leucine number alone gates growth.

**Attempt 2 — does the acute MPS response even predict long-term hypertrophy?** Strongest counter-evidence: Mitchell et al. (2014) and related work found **acute post-exercise MPS correlates poorly with individual hypertrophy over 12-16 weeks**; group-level MPS signals do not reliably rank who grows.
**Result:** model **survives with an explicit boundary.** This is exactly why the outcome is classified **Complex** and the time horizon caps predictive use at hours-to-weeks: MPS is a valid acute mechanism to *derive the practice*, not a long-term growth predictor. Do not use MPS to forecast an individual's monthly gain — instrument instead.

## Confidence

- **High** on the core mechanism (the AND-logic at mTORC1, the leucine threshold).
- **Medium** on the exact timing of the refractory period and per-meal optimization.

## Boundaries and doubts

- This model applies to **skeletal muscle**. Do not extrapolate to cardiac muscle, neurons, or tumor cells — mTOR plays a different role there.
- **Chronic systemic mTOR activation** (high protein all day, no training) is linked to longevity risk per Longo's research. Exact mechanism and magnitude of risk: ⬜ black box, unresolved.
- **Anabolic resistance** develops after age ~60 — the leucine threshold may rise and the mechanical signal may need to be stronger.
- Other known mTOR inputs: energetic status (AMPK as inhibitor), hypoxia (REDD1), cortisol — interaction with the training signal: ❓ unresolved.
- **Scale/context edges:** the model operates at molecular-to-fiber signaling driving a single-fiber / muscle-level adaptation — it is **not** a whole-organism body-composition model. Validity window: a **trained-to-moderately-trained adult under sufficient mechanical stimulus** (sets near failure, RIR 0-3) with adequate energy. Degrades in **energy deficit** (AMPK dominant) and with **anabolic resistance** (age ~60+, sarcopenia, inactivity, disease), where the leucine threshold rises and the mechanical dose must increase.
