# Healthy Teeth

**Two distinct diseases — different etiology, different prevention levers, NOT interchangeable.** Conflating them is the classic error ("I cut sugar, so I don't need to brush" → avoids cavities, gets gum disease).

## Disease 1 — Caries (cavities)

**Etiology:** fermentable carbohydrates (sugars: sucrose/glucose/fructose **and** cooked refined starch) × **frequency** → acidogenic bacteria (S. mutans, lactobacilli) → acid → enamel demineralization. Frequency of exposure matters as much as amount.

**Prevention levers (by evidence strength):**
1. **Fluoride** (topical, toothpaste) — strongest, best-evidenced anti-caries agent: remineralizes enamel → fluorapatite (acid-resistant), inhibits bacterial metabolism. "Spit, don't rinse." → Toothpaste is the fluoride vehicle (the functional point); freshness is cosmetic.
2. **Low frequency of fermentable carbs** (sugars + refined starch) — near-eliminates caries risk. Frequency > amount.
3. Saliva (flow/buffering) — protective; dry mouth raises risk.

**Separate threat — acid erosion:** acidic foods/drinks (lemon, vinegar/ACV, citrus, soda) dissolve enamel **directly** (not bacterial). → dilute, straw, rinse, don't sip.

## Disease 2 — Periodontal disease (gums → bone → tooth loss)

**Etiology:** dental **biofilm** (plaque) = organized bacterial community in a self-made matrix; forms from saliva + bacteria **independent of diet/sugar**. Subgingival biofilm (LPS/endotoxin) triggers the **host inflammatory response** (cytokines, MMPs, osteoclasts) — and it's the **host's own inflammation that destroys collagen attachment + alveolar bone** (bacteria initiate, host executes). Gingivitis (reversible) → periodontitis (largely irreversible: pockets, bone loss). Undisturbed biofilm **mineralizes** (Ca/PO₄ from saliva & crevicular fluid) into **calculus** (~2 weeks) — a rough, non-removable scaffold that retains more biofilm and sustains inflammation.

**Prevention levers (by evidence strength):**
1. **Mechanical biofilm disruption** — brushing (mechanical, for gums) **+ interdental cleaning** (floss / water flosser). Most disease starts **between** teeth. This is the core lever; **sugar control does NOT substitute** (biofilm forms regardless of diet).
2. **Professional scaling** — periodically removes calculus (the non-removable biofilm reservoir).
3. **Not smoking** — biggest modifiable periodontitis risk.

## The model's point (don't conflate)
- **Sugar↓ + fluoride → caries.** **Mechanical cleaning → gums.** Not interchangeable.
- Brushing's anti-**caries** benefit is mostly the **fluoride**; its anti-**gum** benefit is the **mechanical** disruption — that's why both fluoride AND mechanical cleaning are needed.

## Scale of simplicity
"How do I keep my teeth?" is complex (chemistry, bacteria, host immunity, saliva, genetics). The model makes it simple by coarse-graining to **two independent disease processes**, each collapsing to its own dominant lever: **caries** → *fluoride + fermentable-carb frequency* (tooth-surface scale); **perio** → *mechanical biofilm disruption* (gum-sulcus scale). The simplifying move is refusing to treat "oral health" as one thing — split it in two and each half becomes a short, deductible checklist.

**Usefulness test:** the two-lever split pays off only while the two commonest diseases are the whole story. Where saliva/genetics/systemic disease dominate — or for the other oral diseases entirely — the split stops simplifying and you need diagnosis/specialist, not the levers (see Boundaries).

## Cynefin domain
- **Mechanism — Complicated (ordered):** acid → demineralization; biofilm → host inflammation → attachment/bone loss. Well-characterized causal chains; the levers follow deductively.
- **Outcome — Complex:** who actually loses a tooth (and when) is emergent — saliva, genetics, host inflammatory set-point, adherence, life stress (bruxism) interact. Same hygiene → different outcomes across people. Don't lend mechanistic confidence to the individual long-run outcome; instrument it.

## Time horizon (Lyapunov)
- **Caries:** demineralization/remineralization balance shifts over **weeks–months**; a cavity forms over **months–years**. Fluoride/frequency changes are readable on that scale.
- **Perio:** biofilm mineralizes to calculus in **~2 weeks** (the actionable window — hence daily disruption); gingivitis→periodontitis→bone loss plays out over **years–decades**.
- Beyond these windows prediction decays (host response, saliva, adherence drift) → **stop forecasting, start instrumenting:** dentist exam + probing depths + bitewings on a fixed cadence, not "I brush so I'm fine."

## Systemic note — correlation vs causation
Periodontitis is **associated** with atherosclerosis/CVD, but **causation to hard endpoints is NOT established**: heavy shared confounding (smoking, diabetes), Mendelian randomization largely **null**, treatment improves surrogates (CRP, endothelial function) but **no RCT shows fewer cardiac events** (AHA: association, not causal). → Motivate oral hygiene by **keeping your teeth/gums** (solid causal chain), not by heart protection (unproven bonus).

## Falsification — the serious attempt to break it
**Strongest challenge: is the "fluoride, not mechanical cleaning, prevents caries" claim actually true?** If plaque removal by brushing were what stops cavities, the whole two-lever split collapses into "just brush." **Counter-evidence sought:** Cochrane reviews of toothbrushing *without* fluoride show **little to no caries reduction**, while fluoride toothpaste vs placebo shows a large, consistent effect — and community water fluoridation cut caries with **no mechanical component at all**. → Claim **survives, sharpened:** brushing's anti-caries value is the fluoride *vehicle*, not the scrubbing. (The mechanical lever remains real but for *perio*, not caries.)

**Second challenge: could the biofilm→host-destruction chain be wrong — is it just "more bacteria = worse gums"?** If bacterial load alone drove bone loss, immunomodulation wouldn't matter and the host-executes framing would be false. **Counter-evidence sought:** smokers get *more* severe periodontitis with *less* visible plaque/inflammation (blunted vascular response yet worse destruction), and host-response variation (diabetes, genetic IL/cytokine profiles) predicts outcome independent of plaque score. → Host-inflammation framing **survives** — load initiates, host severity determines destruction.

**Third (already resolved above): perio → CVD hard endpoints.** Attempted the causal claim, MR largely null, no RCT reduces cardiac events → **failed as causal**, kept only as association. Recorded in Systemic note.

## Boundaries — what this model does NOT cover
- **Only the 2 most common diseases.** Misses: **bruxism/clenching** (wear, cracks — stress/sleep-driven), **abrasion** (aggressive brushing / abrasive paste), **TMJ** disorders, **malocclusion/bite**, **oral cancer** (screening, esp. with risk factors), endodontic/pulp issues, sensitivity, fractures.
- **Scale/context edges:** operates from biofilm → tissue → whole-mouth; **not systemic** (perio→CVD downgraded above). Shifts hard with dry mouth (meds, IF, dehydration, mouth-breathing), the childhood fluorosis/enamel window, smoking, and diabetes.
- **Assumes a typical host.** Genetic susceptibility (immune response, enamel quality) and **saliva** (flow/buffer/antimicrobial — reduced by meds, dehydration, fasting, mouth-breathing, age) strongly modify BOTH diseases. Some get perio despite good hygiene; some get caries despite low sugar (dry mouth).
- **Lever ranking is population-level** — individual optimal weighting varies (good saliva + low sugar → less fluoride needed; dry mouth → more).
- **Prevention framework, not diagnosis/treatment.** An existing cavity / deep pocket needs a dentist, not just levers.
- **Fluoride has an upper bound** — protective topically at toothpaste dose; excess (esp. systemic, childhood) → fluorosis. "More" isn't linearly better.
- **Adherence & technique sit outside the model** — best levers fail if inconsistent or done wrong (bad brushing → abrasion + missed zones).
## Confidence
- **High:** the two-disease split; sugar/fluoride → caries; biofilm/mechanical → perio; fluoride as the top anti-caries agent; biofilm forms independent of sugar.
- **Established-null / uncertain:** perio → CVD *causation* (treat as unproven).

## Practice
- Fluoride toothpaste, **spit don't rinse**.
- **Interdental cleaning daily** (irrigator; floss may edge it on tight contacts).
- Keep refined-sugar/starch **frequency** low.
- **Acid erosion:** dilute acidic drinks (lemon / ACV / soda), straw, rinse, don't sip.
- Periodic **professional scaling**.
