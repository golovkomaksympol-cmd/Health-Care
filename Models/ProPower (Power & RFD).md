# ProPower — Power & Rate of Force Development

Axis #3 of the 9 (`_core/Models/Training Adaptations (Galpin 9).md`), given its own file because it's programmed differently from everything else. **Power = force × velocity; the trained quantity is RFD (rate of force development)** — *how fast* force rises, not its ceiling. Primarily a **neural** adaptation, expressed **through a movement pattern** — so it has an internal *pattern axis* that training must respect.

## Mechanism

**It's the speed of activation, not more units.** Strength adds *how many* units and *how hard*; power trains *how fast you switch them on*:

- **Rate coding** — motor neurons fire at very high **initial** frequency, including **doublets/triplets** (bursts of 2–3 impulses at ~100–200 Hz at contraction onset) → steepens the front of the force curve = higher RFD. *The dominant power adaptation.*
- **Early, synchronous recruitment of high-threshold (type II) units** — in strength you *recruit* the fast units; in power you recruit them **up front, in the first milliseconds**, not by ramping.
- **Intermuscular coordination / timing** — sequencing agonists, cutting antagonist co-contraction.
- **Stretch-shortening cycle (SSC)** — stored **elastic energy** in tendon + the **stretch reflex** add force "for free" (jumps, swings). Adaptation = tendon stiffness + reflex tuning.
- **Preferential to type II fibers** — which **decline fastest with age** → power is the axis that erodes first and matters most for fall-arrest / getting up.

**"Different neurons" ≈ different motor programs.** The same motor neuron serves a muscle across tasks; what's task-specific is the **central program** (cortical sequence + timing) plus per-joint SSC tuning. So power is learned **per pattern** — it's a *track the CNS plays*, and each pattern is a different track.

**Velocity-specificity.** Adaptation is specific to the **speed/load zone** trained: light-ballistic shifts the **high-velocity** end of the force–velocity curve, heavy-explosive the **high-force** end. Even inside one pattern, "fast" and "heavy" build different points.

**Trained by INTENT to move maximally fast, never to fatigue** — fatigue drops velocity, and velocity *is* the signal. Grinding power erases the very adaptation.

## Trainable power patterns (the catalog)

Power is expressed through a pattern, and **transfer clusters by biomechanical family** (low transfer across dissimilar patterns — an explosive vertical press barely helps a horizontal hip snap). Train power in **each pattern you actually need**; one explosive lift does not cover the vector. Map each to the real-world task it protects:

| Pattern | Power expressions | SSC? | Real-world task protected |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Vertical push (triple-ext up)** | push press, jerk, thruster, jump squat, box/vertical jump | ✓ (jumps) | get up fast, place/throw overhead |
| **Hip-hinge / posterior ballistic** | KB/DB swing, power clean, broad jump, sprint start | ✓ | catch a fall, sprint, hip-snap lift |
| **Horizontal push** | plyo/clap push-up, med-ball chest pass, bench throw | ✓ | shove, push off, arrest a forward fall |
| **Horizontal pull** | explosive row, band-resisted pull, med-ball backward throw | partial | yank, pull-to-safety |
| **Rotation / anti-rotation** | med-ball rotational (scoop/shot) throw, cable chop (fast) | ✓ | throwing, twisting, sport, trunk transfer |
| **Locomotor / reactive (SSC-dominant)** | bounding, pogo/skip hops, depth jumps, change-of-direction | ✓✓ | gait recovery, stumble-catch, fast walk |
| **Landing / deceleration (eccentric power)** | drop landings, decel drills, snap-downs | absorb | **land safely, arrest a fall** (most neglected) |

## Scale of simplicity

"How do I stay powerful / not fall at 80?" is intractable at the physiology level. This model coarse-grains it to **two levers: (1) explosive INTENT (train RFD, not fatigue), (2) the right PATTERNS (train the ones your life needs, by family).** At that scale "am I keeping power?" collapses to "am I moving *something* maximally fast in each pattern I care about, and is its own speed-metric holding?"

**Usefulness test:** drop below to motor-unit physiology to prescribe → intractable and useless at the gym. Stay at the **intent × pattern** scale.

## Cynefin domain

- **Mechanism (explosive intent → RFD in a trained pattern):** **Complicated** — ordered, derivable, testable per pattern.
- **Outcome (does trained power actually prevent *this* person's real fall):** **Complex** — a real stumble is chaotic, multi-planar, unrehearsed; transfer is pattern/context-specific. Don't lend the clean mechanism's confidence to "I train jumps so I won't fall." Instrument function; train reactive/landing patterns, not just concentric.

## Time horizon (Lyapunov)

- Neural power adaptations appear **fast (days→weeks)** — but also **detrain fast** (power fades quicker than strength or hypertrophy). 
- Precise prediction holds one step: *this stimulus → this pattern's RFD.* **Cross-pattern transfer is NOT forecastable** — so **instrument each pattern separately** (jump height, bar velocity, throw distance) on a fixed cadence; don't assume power built in one pattern persists or spreads.

## Practice

- **Fresh, low reps (1–5), maximal intent, full recovery, NEVER to fatigue.** Power goes first in the session.
- **Cover the patterns you need** (Decathlon → pattern map above), not one favourite lift. Include **SSC/plyometric** and **landing/deceleration**, not only concentric.
- **Pick the load zone by intent** (velocity-specific): ballistic light-moderate (~30–60% 1RM) moved maximally, or heavier with intent to accelerate.
- **Test each pattern on its own metric** — don't infer one from another.

## Confidence

- **High:** power = neural + RFD; rate-coding/doublets; SSC; type-II preferential + fastest age-related loss; power detrains fast; velocity- and pattern-specificity.
- **Medium:** the exact size of any **general** ballistic-intent transfer (small but nonzero); optimal load per pattern.

## Falsification

**Attack 1 — "power is just strength expressed fast; get strong and power follows; there's no separate adaptation."** Counter: RFD and 1RM **dissociate** — ballistic training raises RFD with little 1RM change; masters athletes lose **power faster than strength**; velocity-specificity is repeatable. → **Survives, qualified by a strength FLOOR:** below a strength threshold (novice/weak), simply getting stronger *does* raise power (force is a term in P=F×v); **above** that floor, power must be trained for *velocity specifically*. So "just get strong" is right for the weak, wrong for the already-strong.

**Attack 2 — "specificity is overstated; general athleticism transfers broadly."** Counter: transfer studies show power transfer is **narrow** — a heavy squat modestly aids a vertical jump (shared triple-extension), but unrelated patterns barely move. → **Specificity survives;** the general-intent transfer is real but small — you still must train each pattern.

**Result — survives as a partly-separable, pattern-specific neural axis.** "Train strength, ignore speed and patterns" fails above the strength floor; the load-bearing rules (explosive intent, per-pattern training, velocity-specificity, fatigue-avoidance) hold.

## Boundaries

- **Distinct from hypertrophy (muscle unit) and max-strength (force ceiling)** — overlaps but separable (Galpin #3).
- **Transfer is narrow** — don't assume one power lift covers the vector; program the patterns.
- **Needs a strength + skill base first** — you load a pattern you *own* (`_core/Models/Skill & Technique (Galpin 1).md`); power on an unowned or fatigued pattern raises injury risk.
- **Fatigue-sensitive** — done tired or to failure it destroys the stimulus *and* raises injury risk.
- **Detrains fast** — needs regular exposure to hold.
