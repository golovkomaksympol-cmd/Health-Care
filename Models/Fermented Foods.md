# Fermented Foods

**Task: pick a *simple, reliable* way to actually get live fermented food in the modern world.** The payload (why bother) lives in `Gut Microbiome.md` — fermented foods are its clearest diversity-raising lever. This model is about **acquisition**, and it's a direct application of `Modern Food Environment.md`: the default store "fermented" product is a fake.

## Mechanism

- **The payload:** live microbes + microbial metabolites + a partly pre-digested matrix. Fermentation also raises bioavailability, lowers antinutrients, and generates some vitamins (K2, certain B). Diversity signal: see `Gut Microbiome.md`.
- **The modern-world catch (parent model in action):** the *default* supermarket "fermented" product delivers **none of the payload** because it's:
  - **Pasteurized / heat-treated → dead** (most shelf-stable sauerkraut, most "pickles" are just **vinegar-brined**, not fermented), or
  - **Sugar-loaded** (flavoured kefir/yogurt, sweetened kombucha).
  The industry sells the *word*, not the *function*. Naive selection fails — exactly the Modern Food Environment thesis.
- **Live-vs-dead markers:** found **refrigerated** (not shelf-stable), labelled **"raw / unpasteurized / live cultures,"** **no vinegar** as the primary brine, **little/no added sugar**, short ingredient list (vegetable + salt), often visibly active (fizz/cloudy brine).
- **The method fork — buy-live vs make:**
  - **Buy:** reliable *only if* you select for the markers above; costlier; limited range.
  - **Make:** **lacto-fermented vegetables are the simplest, safest, cheapest entry** — cabbage/veg + ~2% salt by weight, kept submerged and anaerobic. It is **safe by design**: salt + anaerobic conditions select for lactic-acid bacteria, whose acid drops pH < 4.6 and **excludes pathogens** (self-protecting). Near-zero equipment (a jar). Kefir (grains + milk) is the simplest dairy route; kombucha is more finicky.

## Scale of simplicity

Hundreds of products, real-vs-fake, buy-vs-make — intractable if you optimise it product by product. This model coarse-grains to **one productive method + one selection rule**: **make lacto-fermented vegetables (salt + veg + jar)** as the default, and when buying, read for **"refrigerated + unpasteurized + no vinegar/sugar."** The whole decision collapses to *"ferment some cabbage, or read three words on a fridge label."*

**Usefulness test:** optimising strain counts or SCFA yield per product is below-scale and pointless — the model works at the **live-vs-dead, make-the-simplest-one** scale.

## Cynefin domain

- **Mechanism (salt + anaerobic → lactic fermentation → safe, live product):** **Clear/Complicated** — a controlled, self-stabilising process; reliable and repeatable.
- **Outcome (does it improve *my* microbiome/health):** **Complex** — individual, per `Gut Microbiome.md`. Instrument, don't predict.

## Time horizon (Lyapunov)

Fermentation runs over **days→weeks** (kraut ~1–4 weeks by taste, then refrigerate to slow it). The health effect operates on the microbiome's **weeks→months** clock (see `Gut Microbiome.md`). Nothing to forecast — make it a standing habit and let the ecosystem respond.

## Practice

- **Default method — lacto-fermented vegetables:** ~**2% salt by weight**, keep everything **under the brine** (anaerobic), room temp **1–3 weeks**, taste, then fridge. Safe by design (salt + anaerobic + acid).
- **Buy-side rule (when not making):** **refrigerated shelf** + **"raw / unpasteurized / live cultures"** + **vinegar not the main brine** + **low/no added sugar.**
- **Rotate a few types** (kraut, kimchi, kefir) for a broader input.

## Confidence

- **High:** lacto-fermentation safety and simplicity; the pasteurized/sugar "fake" problem; the buy-side markers.
- **Medium:** magnitude of the health benefit and which ferment is "best" (defers to `Gut Microbiome.md`).

## Falsification

**Attack 1 — "store-bought is fine; home fermentation is needless effort."** Counter: most shelf-stable "fermented" products are **pasteurized (dead) or vinegar/sugar** — the live-culture function is simply absent. But **some refrigerated live products do exist**, so "you *must* make it" is too strong. → **Qualified:** you don't have to make it, but you **must select for live** — and because selection in the modern store is unreliable (parent model), **home-made is the failure-proof default.**

**Attack 2 — "do the microbes even survive stomach acid / matter?"** Some don't survive transit. → **Survives:** the **diversity/anti-inflammatory benefit of fermented *foods* (Wastyk 2021) does not hinge on colonisation** — metabolites, cell-wall components, and the matrix contribute regardless. Benefit is of the *food*, not a guaranteed *engraftment*.

**Result — survives, qualified:** acquire live ferment; home lacto-veg is the robust default because the modern store can't be trusted to sell you the function.

## Boundaries

- **Not probiotic capsules** — that's a different tool and model (`Probiotics.md`); this is *food*.
- **Safety is method-dependent:** lacto-veg fermentation is safe by design, but **low salt, non-anaerobic setups, or oil-based garlic infusions can be unsafe** (botulism risk in anaerobic oil) — follow the salt-% + submersion rules.
- **Acid / histamine sensitivity:** very acidic or high-histamine ferments can irritate a sensitive or inflamed gut — reintroduce gradually (individual tuning lives in the patient profile, not here).
- **Benefit magnitude is uncertain** — defer to `Gut Microbiome.md`; this model only solves *acquisition*.
