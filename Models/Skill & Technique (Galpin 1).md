# Skill & Technique (Galpin adaptation #1)

Axis #1 of the 9 (`_core/Models/Training Adaptations (Galpin 9).md`). The **neuromuscular foundation** — every other axis is loaded *through* a movement pattern, so a bad pattern degrades everything built on top.

## Mechanism

Skill = the nervous system producing a movement **correctly, coordinated, efficiently**. It is a **neural / motor-learning adaptation** (myelination, motor-unit recruitment & rate-coding patterns, inter-muscular timing) — **not a tissue adaptation**. It is highly **task-specific**: squat skill ≠ hinge skill ≠ throw skill; transfer between patterns is minimal.

Skill has a **readiness foundation** — the order matters (Gray Cook's pyramid / "movement before strength"):

**Mobility → Stability / Motor control → Skill → (load it: strength, power, …)**

- **Mobility** — the joint ROM must physically be *available*.
- **Stability / motor control** — you must *own and control* that ROM under low load.
- **Skill** — the coordinated, efficient pattern.

If you load skill/strength **on top of absent mobility** → the body borrows range elsewhere (compensation) → grooves a dysfunctional pattern + injury. **On top of absent stability** → you load a pattern you can't control → injury. This is "fitness on dysfunction" (Cook). **Fix the limiter lowest in the chain first** (this is the prerequisite-tree / constraint logic — see Decision Principles).

## Scale of simplicity

"Why does this movement fail / how do I fix it?" is a tangle of joints, muscles, timing, and tissue. The model makes it simple by coarse-graining to the **movement pattern** (squat, hinge, press, gait), then to one ordered question: *what is the lowest unmet prerequisite — mobility, then stability, then skill?* At that scale a messy diagnosis collapses to "fix the lowest limiter first." You deliberately do **not** prescribe at the muscle or motor-unit level — the pattern is the simplifying unit.

**Usefulness test:** the model pays off only when a *pattern-level limiter* is the story. If the problem is really tissue (hypertrophy, a torn structure) or acute pain/guarding distorts the pattern, this scale no longer makes the system simple — switch models (see Boundaries).

## Cynefin domain

- **Mechanism (motor learning, readiness ordering):** **Complicated** — the causal chain (available ROM → controlled ROM → coordinated pattern → load) is ordered and diagnosable by an expert; the limiter can be screened and the fix is knowable.
- **Outcome (does fixing the pattern prevent injury / transfer to performance in *this* body):** **Complex** — injury and long-run performance are emergent from load history, tissue tolerance, sleep, psychology, sport demands. Do not lend the mechanism's confidence to the claim "correct pattern ⇒ no injury." Screen and correct, then instrument outcomes.

## Time horizon (Lyapunov)

- Motor-learning consolidation is predictable over **days → weeks** for a given pattern with high-frequency quality reps; re-testing a screened limiter after a focused block (**~4–8 weeks**) is reliable.
- Beyond that — whether the improved pattern *holds under real load/fatigue and reduces injury* over **months–years** — prediction decays. **Switch from predicting to instrumenting:** re-screen periodically, track pattern quality under load, watch for compensation/pain tripwires rather than assuming the fix persists.

## Practice

- **Screen the foundation before loading:** find the limiter (mobility vs stability) per joint/pattern and fix *that* first — don't add load over it.
- **Train skill fresh, never fatigued:** motor learning favors many **high-quality reps**, not grinding. Practice technique early in the session, low fatigue, perfect reps.
- **High frequency, low fatigue** — skill consolidates with frequent exposure, not with soreness.
- Skill is the **substrate for every other axis** — you always load a *pattern*; secure the pattern first.

## Confidence

- **High** on motor-learning principles and on movement-readiness ordering (well established in PT / motor development).
- **Medium** on the exact mobility-vs-stability limiter for a given person — must be diagnosed, not assumed.

## Falsification

Serious attempt to break *this* model — attacking its weakest load-bearing claims:

1. **"Screening the movement foundation predicts and prevents injury."** The strongest counter-evidence: the **FMS / Cook movement-screen literature is weak as an injury predictor.** Systematic reviews and prospective cohorts (e.g. Bunn et al. 2019; Moran et al. 2017; Dorrel et al.) find the composite FMS score has **poor predictive validity** — the popular "≤14 → high injury risk" cutoff shows low sensitivity/specificity and doesn't generalize across populations. So the *predictive/screening* claim, as sold, **fails**.
2. **The "mobility → stability → skill" fixed ordering.** It is largely a **clinical heuristic (Cook's pyramid), not RCT-validated**; the strict linear sequence is contestable — stability and skill co-develop, and some patterns are trained effectively without first "maxing" mobility.

**Result — model survives, qualified (scope narrowed):** The *screening-as-injury-predictor* claim is **rejected** — do not treat a movement screen as an injury-risk oracle. But the model's actual load-bearing core is different: (a) skill is a **task-specific neural adaptation** — strongly supported by motor-learning science; (b) **you cannot load range you don't have without compensating** — a near-mechanical truth (compensation is observable, not statistical); (c) **train skill fresh, high-frequency/low-fatigue** — well supported. These survive. The ordering is kept as a **prioritization heuristic ("fix the lowest limiter first"), not a validated injury-prediction law.** Net effect: the model is retained for *deriving practice*, demoted for *predicting injury outcomes* (consistent with the Complex outcome domain above).

## Boundaries & caveats

- Skill is **specific** — don't expect a clean carry-over between unrelated patterns.
- "Core stability" specifics are debated; the *principle* (control before load) is robust, the exact drills less so.
- **Scale/context edges:** covers voluntary CNS-driven patterns — not reflexive/autonomic control, not tissue adaptation. The binding limiter (mobility vs stability) is individual, per person and per joint. Most valid at low-to-moderate fatigue; degrades under high fatigue, pain, or acute injury where guarding distorts the pattern.
