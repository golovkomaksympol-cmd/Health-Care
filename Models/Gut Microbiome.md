# Gut Microbiome

The colonic bacterial ecosystem — what it is, how it's disturbed, and how it actually recovers. The **facts about the system.** The *intervention* people misuse against it lives separately in `_core/Models/Probiotics.md`.

## Mechanism

- **The ecosystem:** the colon holds **several hundred bacterial species** (commonly cited 500–1000; ~150–170 dominant per person) — a dense, self-organizing community, largely unique per person.
- **Antibiotic disturbance:** a course can drop diversity and let opportunists (incl. *C. difficile*) expand — **but not always, and not always symptomatically.** Antibiotic-associated diarrhea (AAD) hits a *minority* of courses (~5–35%, agent-dependent); most people get no gut symptom. **Disruption ≠ disease.**
- **Recovery is mostly spontaneous:** the bulk of the community rebounds over **weeks → a few months** on its own. Some taxa can stay depressed **6 months+ or be lost**, especially with repeated courses.
- **What actually feeds it (the real restoration levers):**
  1. **Fiber / MACs (microbiota-accessible carbohydrates) + resistant starch** — the *substrate* colonic bacteria eat. **Correction to the intuitive story:** fiber doesn't "escort sugars down to the colon" — simple sugars and digestible starch are absorbed high in the small intestine and never reach the colon anyway; **fermentable fiber and resistant starch are themselves the food that survives** (humans can't digest them, so they arrive intact). 
  2. **Fermented foods** (sauerkraut, kimchi, kefir, live yogurt) — live-microbe + metabolite input; the intervention with the clearest **diversity-raising** signal (Wastyk/Sonnenburg 2021).

## Scale of simplicity

A several-hundred-species ecosystem is intractable at the species level. This model makes it simple by coarse-graining to **the ecosystem's inputs and disturbances** — substrate in, disturbance minimized — not its composition. The handle is *"garden the soil, don't count the species."*

**Usefulness test:** drop to "which of the ~800 species do I have / want" and the system is hopelessly complex again — that's below-scale. Stay at the substrate/disturbance level.

## Cynefin domain

- **Mechanism — Complicated:** antibacterial → ↓ diversity; fermentable fiber → SCFA. Ordered, derivable.
- **Outcome — Complex:** *which* taxa this person loses and recovers, and when, is emergent and individual. Instrument (stool/symptoms), don't forecast composition.

## Time horizon (Lyapunov)

Disturbance plays out over **days → weeks**; recovery over **weeks → months**; specific composition is **not forecastable** past the near term → **instrument via stool/symptoms**, not an imagined species count.

## Practice

- **Feed it:** high **fiber / MACs + resistant starch**, plus **fermented foods**.
- **Minimize unnecessary disturbance** (don't take antibiotics you don't need).
- **Let spontaneous recovery run** — the ecosystem re-seeds itself from its own survivors; your job is substrate, not installation.

## Confidence

- **High:** ecosystem scale; SCFA/butyrate biology; spontaneous recovery as the norm; fiber/RS as the substrate.
- **Medium:** that fermented foods beat fiber for *diversity* specifically (single 10-week study).

## Falsification

**Attempt — "fiber is the dominant restoration lever."** Strongest counter: the **Wastyk/Sonnenburg (2021)** arm found that **high-fiber over 10 weeks did NOT raise microbiome diversity** (it shifted *function* — enzyme repertoire — and, in low-diversity people, transiently raised inflammatory markers), whereas **fermented foods did raise diversity and lowered inflammation.** → **Fiber's role qualified, not broken:** fiber reliably *feeds* the community and drives SCFA/function (mechanism intact), but "fiber quickly restores *diversity*" is **downgraded** — for a diversity signal, fermented foods have the better evidence. Both levers kept, roles clarified.

## Boundaries

- **Colonic bacteria only** — doesn't cover the small-bowel microbiome (SIBO), fungal/viral components, or oral/skin microbiomes.
- **Doesn't cover** IBD / IBS / SIBO-specific management.
- **Individual composition is unpredictable** — no generic target profile.
